## Headline findings

### F1 — There was a regime change
Nepal had a Gen-Z uprising in September 2025, killing 74+ and forcing PM Oli out. Interim govt under former CJ Sushila Karki for ~6 months. **March 5, 2026 election: Balen Shah's RSP won 182/275 seats** (first single-party majority since 1999). **Sworn in March 27, 2026.** Finance Minister: **Swarnim Wagle** (World Bank alumnus). Anti-corruption mandate.

This was the dominant context my prior knowledge missed.

### F2 — The Apr 1 + Apr 5 NEPSE crashes had different proximate triggers

**Apr 1 (−2.62%, all 11 sectors red, hydropower-led):**
- Primary driver: FM Wagle's "100-day Integrated Digital Asset Registry" plan + asset-scrutiny posture (P=0.45)
- Secondary: Bhatta probe disclosure Mar 31 (P=0.20)

**Apr 5 (−3.79%, 261/268 stocks red, reinsurance-led):**
- Primary driver: Sulav Agrawal arrest (Apr 4) extending the Bhatta scandal to a second business group (P=0.55)
- HRL hit −10% lower circuit on Rs 582M turnover

The cleanest forensic fingerprint: turnover migration from hydropower (Apr 1, AKJCL led) → reinsurance (Apr 5, HRL led). **Different drivers, not the same event extended.**

### F3 — The "rapidly down" asset was a misattribution
"The sugar market" tag decoded: SEBON probe names **Shubhi Agrawal** as a facilitator. Nepali traders associate "Agrawal" with Reliance Sugar Mills (Birgunj Agrawal family). Different family — same surname → mental shortcut.

**Final ranked identification:**
- NRIC (Nepal Re-Insurance) — 40% probability
- HRL (Himalayan Reinsurance) — 30% probability
- MKHL (Mai Khola Hydropower) — 20% probability (lock-in expiry May 2)
- Other / unknown — 10%

There is no live tradeable NEPSE sugar stock. SRS (Shree Ram Sugar Mills) shut in 2020.

### F4 — The persistence signal misses are sector-rotation flood, not regime change
Probability ranking on why NABIL/EBL persistence signal missed 5-in-a-row:
- **H2: Flight to safety into commercial banks during scandal** — 40%
- H4: NABIL Q3 earnings front-running (+33.92% YoY) — 20%
- H1: Regime change post-election — 15%
- H3: Original 2025 was lucky clustering (already in L-012) — 10%
- H5: Random noise on N=5 — 8%
- Other — 7%

Cleanest evidence: EBL Apr 6 case had **bad** Q3 (−3.71% YoY) and **still** went +0.73% during a −6% market crash. That requires exogenous buying. Persistent retail/HNI sellers were absorbed.

### F5 — The prosecution is selectively scoped (Dhakal anomaly)
P(prosecution selective) = 70%.

The arrests skip the *current* FNCCI president (Chandra Prasad Dhakal, IME Group chair) and target the *previous* president (Golchha, 2020-23). Either Dhakal's IME tenure is statistically clean while Golchha's was systemically dirty (possible but convenient), or the prosecutorial filter is cohort-political, not institutional.

The May 2 cabinet purge of 1,594 politically-appointed officials — pre-Mar-26 appointees — confirms institutional-level selection at scale.

### F6 — Cooperative sector is chronic-but-stable, not imminent crisis
Originally I had P(coop implosion 12mo) at 30%. Updated to **16%**.

**Why down:** Rs 87.89B confirmed embezzled, ~60,000 depositors locked out. BUT institutional plumbing (Cooperative First Amendment Ordinance Apr 30, Revolving Fund, NCRA, Problematic Cooperative Management Committee at coopmc.gov.np) is in place. Crises with workout vehicles grind, they don't implode. NRB-DoC regulatory wall protects banks from contagion (no Class A bank publicly named as exposed lender to a failing cooperative).

### F7 — DMLI expanded probe May 1 to 8 insurance/reinsurance companies
New names brought into scope: Guardian Micro Life, Crest Micro Life, Liberty Micro Life, Protective Insurance, Star Micro Insurance, Trust Micro Insurance — plus Himalayan Reinsurance and Nepal Micro Insurance already named. DMLI requested promoter-share premium data — pre-arrest evidence-gathering.

**Every microinsurance ticker on NEPSE now has structural overhang for at least a quarter.**

### F8 — SEBON Chairman Shrestha already resigned (Apr 17)
He had been allegedly Bhatta-appointed (regulatory capture allegation). His resignation absorbed the regulatory-capture pressure without an arrest. Selection panel formed Apr 21 (47 applicants); no replacement announced as of May 4.

### F9 — NEPSE Chairman + CEO BOTH resigned April 27-28
Hemanta Basyal (Chair) + Chudamani Chapagain (CEO). Both resignations approved by FM Wagle. **No replacements announced as of May 4.** Leadership vacuum at the exchange itself, mid-correction.

### F10 — Supreme Court released Golchha on bail (May 1-5 window)
Golchha had been in custody from Apr 23. SC ordered him bailable. **This is a meaningful crack in the prosecution narrative** — either the state's case is procedurally weaker than headlines suggest, or the judiciary is independent enough to push back against prosecution velocity.
