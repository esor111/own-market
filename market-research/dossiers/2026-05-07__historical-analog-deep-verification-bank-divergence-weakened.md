This reframes — but does not collapse — the NABIL conviction. Position size remains 18-20% justified by current Q3 fundamentals; the cohort-leader analog support is weaker than previously framed.

## Verification B — Bank divergence (BIG correction)

### Window-by-window

**India 2018-19 (IL&FS):** PATTERN CONFIRMED
- Yes Bank: peak Rs 404 (Aug 20, 2018) → Rs 167 (Apr 30, 2019) = **−59%**, against Sensex −10% in the acute Sept-Oct window
- PNB: −32% calendar 2018
- HDFC Bank, Kotak Mahindra: qualitatively flat-to-up (specific numbers GAP)
- **Leader-laggard gap: ~30-50 pp**
- Driver: NBFC exposure on loan book — specific, not generic

**Bangladesh 2024-26 (Hasina cleanup):** PATTERN PARTIALLY FALSIFIED
- DSEX: −16.5% in 2024 (peak ~6,178 → close 5,216)
- Banking sector aggregate: −2.5% YTD (banks outperformed DSEX by ~14pp)
- **Islami Bank Bangladesh (the supposed laggard): +115% Aug 6 → Sep 25, 2024** — rallied on cleanup catalyst (removal of S Alam was bullish, not bearish)
- True laggard collapse delayed: First Security Islami Bank → equity zero Nov 2025 (14 months after Hasina ouster)
- **The cleanup catalyst can RALLY the supposed laggard short-term**
- Driver: short-term, cleanup as bullish event for previously-controlled bank; long-term, regulatory grace expiry kills truly insolvent

**Sri Lanka 2022 (Aragalaya):** SOFT FALSIFIER
- ASPI: ~13,000 (mid-2021) → ~7,700-9,000 (early 2023) = ~−40%
- Sampath Bank: all-time low LKR 25.25 on July 8, 2022 (during Aragalaya peak)
- Commercial Bank, HNB: GAP on specifics, named as top ASPI contributors post-recovery
- State banks (BoC, People's): unlisted; absorbed sovereign restructuring losses but invisible in equity prints
- **Listed private banks tracked the index DOWN** — sovereign default = systemic risk hits all listed banks together
- Driver: sovereign exposure, not bank-specific quality

**Vietnam 2022 (Blazing Furnace):** GAP
- VN-Index −42.8% confirmed
- VCB/BID/MBB specific 2022 returns: Yahoo blocked, Investing.com blocked, no clean data retrieved
- VanEck Vietnam ETF (VNM): −43.7% in 2022, almost identical to index — suggests bank divergence was muted within the basket
- **No verification possible from accessible sources**

### Synthesis

**1 of 4 windows clearly confirms. 1 of 4 partially falsifies. 2 of 4 unverifiable.**

The "leader −15-25%, index −40%, laggard −50-60%" pattern I'd been carrying is supported only by India 2018-19. Bangladesh shows the laggard can RALLY on cleanup catalyst short-term. Sri Lanka shows banks can move together with the index. Vietnam is GAP.

### Mechanism reframe

The India case suggests the actual driver is **specific exposure to the cleanup target**, not cohort tag:
- **SBI (state-owned, large): fine.** **PNB (state-owned, large): collapsed.** Same cohort, opposite outcomes — driven by NBFC exposure on loan book.
- **HDFC (corporate-light, retail-heavy): untouched.** **Yes Bank (corporate-heavy, NBFC-exposed): collapsed.** Driven by loan-book composition.

The mechanistic version of the trade thesis ("avoid banks with direct exposure to whatever is being cleaned up") is much better supported than the cohort-tag version ("quality leaders compound").

## Application to NABIL

The reframed question is not "is NABIL a quality leader?" — fundamentals say yes. The right question is:

**"What is NABIL's specific exposure to whatever is being cleaned up in Nepal?"**

What's being cleaned up:
- Bhrikuti broker (broker-side, not bank-side directly)
- Bhatta/Agrawal insurance cohort
- Cooperative sector (Rs 87.89B parliamentary probe)
- Potentially: Bhandari Commission expansion to bank directors with foreign vehicles

NABIL's exposure profile:
- **Direct lending exposure to cleanup-targeted entities**: not visible in disclosures yet (W16 watch — Q3 weekly compilation pending mid-May)
- **Governance exposure**: Nirvana Chaudhary Chairman (Jan 2026) + NB International offshore vehicle structure = exact target pattern of Bhandari Commission. P=20% over 90d (W18)

NABIL's exposure to the cleanup target is **non-zero and concentrated on the governance dimension**, not the loan-book dimension. Different risk profile than "quality leader rides through."

### Key observation: Vietnam's second-arrest acceleration

Trinh Van Quyet (FLC) arrested Mar 29, 2022 — VN-Index already −24% from Jan peak by May 2022.
Truong My Lan (Van Thinh Phat) arrested Oct 6, 2022 — index fell another ~25% to Nov 16 trough at 873.

**The second major arrest accelerated the slide; it did not mark the bottom.**

For NEPSE: Sulav Agrawal arrested Apr 5, 2026 (~32 days ago). If the Vietnam clock applies, the historically-dangerous window is the next 4-6 months — when the second major arrest (Sandip Chachan, Shubhi Agrawal, Raj Bahadur Shah) is likely.

## Process learning (worth flagging)

When the prior research session synthesized historical analogs from training memory, it produced numbers that were **off by 7-10pp on drawdown depth and 12-24 months on recovery time**, and **overstated bank-divergence support by 3x**.
