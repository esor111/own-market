### F3 — Bhrikuti contagion is much smaller than I framed

**Three findings de-risked the contagion concern:**

1. **The Rs 5.11B is broker-to-CLIENT receivables, NOT CDSC clearing-system stress.** Deepak Bhatta alone owes Rs 2.89B; remainder spread across other credit clients. SEBON ordered Bhrikuti to wind down its OWN book — failure was not socialized through CDSC.

2. **CDSC silence is calm-confidence, not information-suppression.** No public notice on guarantee fund deployment, no T+2 → T+3 extension, no position takeover. SEBON used the wind-down path that explicitly keeps CDSC clearing untouched.

3. **Cross-market analogs strongly suggest exchanges DO NOT halt for broker default:**
   - Bangladesh 2010-11 omnibus account scandal: settlement never halted; DGEN fell 21% but exchange operated
   - Karachi 2005 scandal: settlement never halted; index fell 65% but exchange operated
   - Settlement infrastructure is more resilient than headline price

**Bhrikuti was MID-TIER, not systemic.** ~80,000 clients = 1.3% of NEPSE's 6M demat accounts. NOT in top 10 by turnover (top 10 do 44%). Direct turnover-attributable share 3-7%, not 50%.

**Insurance sector took the hit, not brokers.** Hydropower, hotels, manufacturing rallied through April unaffected. No standalone listed broker stocks exist (brokerage exposure is buried in parent bank financials).

### F4 — One residual contagion risk: Rs 5.11B may not be fully collectible

Deepak Bhatta's Rs 2.89B is in police probe → recovery probability materially below 100%. If Bhrikuti's own equity can't cover the gap, **THAT is the path that could eventually force CDSC fund deployment.** It's not 30-day risk — it's 6-12 month risk as the legal recovery process plays out.

**The thesis is REAL, but evidence in NEPSE today is too thin. Stay AVOID on the 8 named. Add SRLI/ALICL/NIL to watch list. Re-evaluate when probe stops expanding for 30+ trading sessions AND a clean candidate prints unambiguously good Q3.**
