# Monsoon Flood Hydro Signal Summary

> Plain-language note for the lab.
> Written 2026-04-12.

## What the idea is

This is **not** a general "monsoon signal."

It is a **damage-confirmed hydropower event signal**:

- a flood happens
- credible public reporting confirms that a specific listed hydropower project's plant or infrastructure is damaged or forced offline
- that company's stock may underperform other hydropower stocks for the next few trading days

The cleanest example is **UPPER** after the September 2024 flood:

- one company
- one major project
- documented physical damage
- long outage

That is the kind of case this signal is trying to capture.

## What the signal is saying

The signal is basically:

**"When a listed hydro company's revenue-generating asset is confirmed damaged by flood, be more bearish on that stock than on undamaged hydros."**

This is not a mood story.
This is not a folklore story.
This is not "all hydros fall in monsoon."

It is a **cash-flow impairment story**.

## Why it could work

Hydropower companies are unusually exposed to physical damage:

- flood damage can destroy intake structures, headworks, roads, or control rooms
- forced outage means no generation
- no generation means lower revenue
- if the damage is underinsured or slow to repair, the hit lasts longer

That creates a plausible short-horizon repricing window after the market learns the damage is real.

## How it helps us

If this idea proves real, it would help in a very specific way:

- not by predicting the whole market
- not by predicting every hydro stock
- but by giving us a **negative event filter** on damaged hydros

In practice that means:

- avoid buying the damaged name too early
- treat that stock as weaker than the hydro basket
- compare damaged vs undamaged hydros instead of asking "will NEPSE go down?"

This is valuable because it is:

- Nepal-specific
- mechanical
- tied to public events
- different from the broker-persistence lane

So even if the edge is modest, it could still be a useful side signal or exclusion rule.

## Does it add extra probability?

Maybe, but we do **not** have the number yet.

The honest answer is:

- **we do not yet know the percentage edge**
- we have a credible mechanism, not a validated hit rate

If it works, the likely shape is **modest edge, not miracle edge**.

Think of it like this:

- baseline: after a flood headline, the market may panic broadly
- better question: does the **specifically damaged hydro** fall more than similar undamaged hydros afterward?

That is the edge we are trying to measure.

The likely realistic outcome, if real, is not "90% accuracy."
It is more likely:

- a higher chance of negative relative return
- or a measurable negative abnormal return over the next few days

That is enough to matter if the effect is clean and repeatable.

## What the current status really is

Current status:

- **interesting**
- **well-motivated**
- **not yet validated**

We should treat it as a **research candidate**, not a live signal.

The right sequence is:

1. sanity-check UPPER after Sep 2024
2. build a damage-confirmation event table
3. compare damaged hydros vs undamaged hydros
4. only then decide whether there is a real signal

## Best one-sentence version

**This is a candidate negative event signal for listed hydropower stocks: when flood damage to a specific project's asset is credibly confirmed, that stock may underperform undamaged hydros for the next few trading days because the market is repricing real revenue loss.**
