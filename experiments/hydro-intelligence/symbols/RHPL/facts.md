# RHPL Facts

## Dated Structured Facts

- **2025-07-08 / 2025-07-09:** credible public reporting said the Rasuwagadhi project halted generation following flood damage after a GLOF / flood event.
- **2025-08-27:** the lock-in period ended for project-affected-area local shares, which matters as a market-liquidity overhang.
- **2025-12-07:** partial restart began, with one of three 37 MW units coming back online.
- **2026-01-03:** strong local reporting said RHPL was supplying **68 MW**, about **61% of installed capacity**, with the remaining restoration targeted for **June 2026**.
- **2026-01-05 to 2026-03-19:** RHPL's own site continued publishing restoration and procurement notices, implying the rebuild was still active rather than completed.
- **2026-01-14 / 2026-02-02:** RHPL completed its AGM cycle and later announced auditor appointment for FY 2082/83.
- **FY 2081/82 annual report (posted 2026-01-05):** revenue from electricity was **Rs 1.148 billion**, but net loss was **Rs 429.5 million**, with heavy finance cost and depreciation.
- **2026-04-10 local tape:** `RHPL` closed at **293.00**. Recent local structure shows **-4.93% over 20 trading sessions**, **-7.28% over 10**, **-3.93% over 5**, with very weak recent participation (**volume ratio 0.29 vs 20-day average**).

## Derived Features

- incomplete recovery story, not finished normalization
- stronger damage-overhang profile than UPPER right now
- structurally useful comparison peer because the market still has to decide whether RHPL is a repaired producer or still a damaged asset
- current tape is weak enough to matter, not just neutral

## Interpretation Caution

- RHPL can inherit narrative force from the Tibet / glacial-flood storyline
- partial restart is real progress, but partial restart is not full restoration
- historical damage context and current weak tape point the same way, which increases conviction but also raises the risk of overconfidence if a full-restart catalyst appears suddenly
